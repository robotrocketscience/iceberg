# Per-mission reliability (L0-10)

## Current

0.80, rolling-5-mission basis. Project-owner-locked in REQUIREMENTS.md v0.6 (Option B from the L0-07/L0-09/L0-10 coordinated revision). **The requirement number is unchanged; status stays closed.**

**2026-07-21 latest+25 — two new failure modes enter the risk register (do not reopen the requirement, but must be carried against it).** (1) **Mothership single-point-of-failure (R189 `R_mothership_threat_split`):** decomposing the relay's chunk-handoff damaging-failure fraction, a stage-weighted ~24 % of damaging handoff failures threaten the reactor-and-chunk-bearing mothership itself — a total-mission-loss event, not a chunk write-off. Central mission-total-loss probability 1.9 % (small on economics) but the stress corner reaches **8.5 % — a program-defining catastrophic risk** that no per-chunk reliability line captures. The berth_bag stage drives it (highest failure probability *and* highest mothership-threat conditional). One counter-intuitive result held: the retry policy is **mothership-safe** (per-retry exposure decays geometrically, ~50-230:1 dominated by retention gain) — the tail is driven by N (chunks per mission), not retry count, putting the fleet-economics push toward high N in direct tension with catastrophic exposure. (2) **Bag dynamics is the one desk-irreducible unknown (R191 `R_bag_dynamics_envelope`):** the bagged 40-tonne load is a 2:1-10:1 host-dominating sloshing mass; contact forces are tractable (≤ 1 kN soft capture) but the coupled attitude problem (slosh in the ACS band, disturbance torque beyond reaction-wheel authority, settling that can exceed the berth window) has a **narrow (~7 %) viable design corner** prescribing an unprecedented bag, and coupled stability cannot be desk-closed — it needs a Basilisk 6-DOF `linearSpringMassDamper` sim (the campaign's highest-value backlog sim). **That sim has now run (`7a716f3`, on archon, bsk 2.10.2):** it confirms R191's viable corner only for an *idealized mean-zero slosh on the heavy monolithic host*, and **overturns the framing of the gate** — the berth *capture transient* injects ~5000 N·m·s of net angular momentum (20 t slosh × 5 cm/s × 5 m arm), ~17× the three-wheel storage capacity, and that momentum is *conserved*: reaction wheels store it but cannot remove it, so all 32 realistic-berth cells saturate and tumble regardless of (T_s, ζ, host). **The binding constraint is angular-momentum management, not torque authority.** A follow-on (`R-berth-momentum-management`, `b3b579c`, 4/4 HELD) sharpened the sim's "thrusters mandatory" reading into a **three-leg architecture trade**, and the earlier "mandatory" phrasing is now an overstatement (corrections-live-forward): (A) **thruster dump** is the robust *fallback* — dump 5000 N·m·s in ~100 s at 50 N·m for ~42 kg of water (0.042 % of wet mass, immaterial per `R_chunk_despin_budget`), the only real cost being N plume firings at the flexible-bag interface; (B) **gravity-gradient desaturation**, unusually strong here because the 40 t bag on a 5 m arm gives ΔI ≈ 1×10⁶ kg·m² → τ_gg ≈ 0.046 N·m at the B-ring, clears one berth injection **propellant- and plume-free in 2.5–8.3 days**, but costs ~300–600 kg of CMG buffer + a days-scale berth-cadence floor (it buys plume-avoidance, *not* mass); (C) **capture-geometry control** — hold v_c·r_off ≤ 0.0075 m²/s (a close ≤2 m / few-mm/s catch) — keeps the injection inside existing reaction-wheel capacity so **no dump is needed at all**, the preferred leg where reachable. **Ranking C < B < A. Vehicle-spec gap flagged:** the current 4×10 kg reaction wheels (~100–150 N·m·s) are 33–50× too small to buffer a nominal berth injection, so the vehicle must adopt geometry-control capture, add a CMG, or fire RCS every berth — the spec (`SATURN-SHIP-SPEC.md`) currently reflects none. **A follow-on plume-impingement round (`a418d4d`, 4/4 HELD) then resolved leg A's open cost — the pivot of the trade:** firing the water RCS to dump the injection is mechanically safe (worst-case plume pressure 2.4 Pa vs the ~10 kPa aerogel limit, 3.6 orders of margin) and ice-clean (grams of intake deposition over 50 berths), and re-perturbation of the just-settled slosh is *avoidable* by keeping the bag in the plume backflow at ≥ 3 m boom standoff (0.22 N·m, 1/250 of the dump; only the plume-core geometry re-excites, at ~22 % of the dump). **So leg A is viable under a thruster-placement / boom-length constraint, and legs B/C drop from *mandatory* to *optional* — retiring only the residual worst-case-dump-axis geometry.** **That last question is now closed (`R-berth-dump-axis-geometry`, `107bf5b`, 4/4 HELD):** because the capture arm is along the boom, the injected momentum is *structurally* perpendicular to the boom, so every required dump axis is in-plane — and in-plane torque couples are realizable from tangential thrusters whose plumes fire sideways (~90° to the forward bag), giving a max bag-plume torque of 0.038 N·m over *all* azimuths (66× under the 2.5 N·m threshold; the retro −z thrusters are the lone plume-danger and are avoidable, the tangential set spanning ± torque about both in-plane axes). **The momentum-management thread therefore CLOSES: leg A (tangential water-RCS dump) is unconditionally viable under a cheap plume-aware control-allocation rule + boom ≥ 3 m, no new hardware; the ~300–600 kg CMG (leg B) and precision-capture (leg C) are retired to optional. One named residual: flight-layout confirmation of tangential authority about both in-plane axes (generic for any 3-axis-couple RCS).** These findings ground R185's low berth_bag stage probability (0.88-0.95) in physics rather than desk anchor, and the R189 mothership saturated-wheel-tumble loss mode is now bounded by an *architectural choice* (worst under leg A if a plume event damages the bag; removed entirely under leg C). **Demonstrator implication:** the LEO handoff-rehearsal gate must measure the berth-contact mothership-threat conditional, **whether the capture concept can hold v_c·r_off ≤ 0.0075 m²/s (leg C — the single number deciding whether any momentum-management hardware or propellant is needed at all)**, the injected angular momentum and RCS/GG momentum-management performance, and the bag's slosh parameters (T_s, ζ) — the numbers this entire risk picture rides on.
