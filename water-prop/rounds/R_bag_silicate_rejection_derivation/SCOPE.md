## The mechanism

The bag is a sublimation still: harvested ring ice (99.7 % water, ~0.3 % silicate by mass) is warmed; water sublimates and the vapor is fed to the thruster; silicate inclusions are exposed at the receding ice front. A liberated silicate grain reaches the thruster (**carryover** = defeats rejection) only if it is both (a) **lofted** off the surface and (b) **transported** by the vapor to the collector. The competition is size-dependent:

1. **Free-molecular (Epstein) vapor drag vs transport decoupling.** In the low-pressure bag the vapor is in molecular-flow. A grain is carried by the vapor only if its Stokes number St = τ_stop·v_flow/L < 1; above a cutoff diameter **d_max = ρ_gas(T)·L / ρ_grain** it decouples and settles/re-impacts (rejected). d_max is set by the vapor density ρ_gas(T) ∝ P_sat(T) — **steeply temperature-dependent**.
2. **Van-der-Waals adhesion** holds fine grains in a lag deposit (F_adh ∝ r) against drag (F_loft ∝ r²) — mechanically rejecting the finest grains *unless* an electrostatic mechanism lofts them.
3. **Electrostatic lofting** (plasma sheath / photoemission dust levitation, well-documented on airless bodies and in cometary comae) lofts the sub-micron/nano tail regardless of adhesion — the residual leak that low-temperature operation cannot suppress.

So the **derived carryover** f_pass = (silicate PSD mass fraction inside the lofted-and-transported band), which the **sub-100-nm inclusion PSD** dominates. The rejection factor is therefore not an intrinsic 10⁻⁴ — it equals a PSD-and-temperature-gated integral.

## Boundary

- **IN:** (a) vapor conditions vs bag temperature {200, 250, 273 K}: P_sat (Marti-Mauersberger ice sublimation), sublimation flux J (Hertz-Knudsen), ρ_gas, thermal speed; the throughput area A_ice to feed ṁ ≈ 0.1–0.5 g/s (matrix RF-ion). ## Pre-registered hypotheses (numeric, falsifiable, before run.py)

**H-K1 [S] — the entrainment cutoff d_max is sub-micron and steeply T-dependent.** d_max = ρ_gas(T)·L/ρ_grain (L = 1 m, ρ_grain = 3000 kg/m³) is ~0.5 nm at 200 K, ~150–250 nm at 250 K, ~1–2 µm at 273 K — spanning ~3.5 orders across a 73 K window, because ρ_gas ∝ P_sat rises ~3700× from 200→273 K. **Falsified** if d_max at 250 K is outside [50 nm, 1 µm], or the T-scaling departs from ρ_gas-linear by more than the P_sat curvature.

**H-K2 [S] — cold-bag operation feeds the thruster within a modest area; throughput does not force warm operation.** The sublimation flux J(T) feeds ṁ = 0.5 g/s from A_ice ≤ ~3 m² at 200 K and ≤ ~0.02 m² at 250 K. So a designer *can* run the bag cold enough to push d_max sub-nanometre (gas entrainment physically off) without starving the thruster. **Falsified** if A_ice > 20 m² at 200 K (cold operation impractical → warm operation forced → gas entrainment unavoidable).

**H-K3 [S, LOAD-BEARING] — the derived rejection factor is PSD-and-temperature-gated, NOT an intrinsic 10⁻⁴; the assumed 10⁻⁴ is a cold-bag + small-nano-tail corner.** The gas-entrainment carryover f_pass_gas = massfrac(PSD < d_max) is near-zero at cold operation (200 K, d_max sub-nm) but **10⁻² – 3×10⁻¹ at warm operation** (273 K, d_max ~µm) even under the plain power-law PSD (no special nano-mode required — a q=3.5 power-law to r_max = 1 µm puts ~25 % of silicate mass below 100 nm). So R11/R_silicate's 10⁻⁴ is defensible **only** if the bag runs cold AND the sub-micron inclusion mass fraction is small — it is not an intrinsic property of the still. **Falsified** if f_pass_gas < 10⁻³ across ALL temperature × PSD combinations (rejection robustly good, assumption safe) or > 10⁻¹ across all (robustly bad regardless of design).

**H-K4 [S] — the electrostatic channel is the residual leak cold operation cannot suppress.** Even at 200 K (gas entrainment off), electrostatic lofting of the sub-d_es nano-tail gives a residual f_pass_es ~ φ_nano·g_geom, bounded ≥ 10⁻³ at Hsu-class φ_nano under a pessimistic geometric capture g_geom ~ 10⁻²–10⁻¹ — so the sub-100-nm PSD remains the load-bearing unknown even for a cold bag. **Falsified** if f_pass_es < 10⁻⁵ for Hsu-class φ_nano at any plausible g_geom (electrostatics negligible; cold operation fully solves it).

**H-K5 [W] — grid-life closes the loop: at Hsu-class rejection R11's own erosion model returns a burn-limiting grid life.** Feeding f_pass at the Hsu/warm corner (~10⁻²) through R11's silicate→grid-wear mapping (16.7 kg silicate-to-grid budget; 0.3 % silicate; M_prop ≈ 690 t → 2070 kg silicate total) consumes the grid **> 1× (fails within the burn)**, whereas at the cold + small-nano-tail corner (~10⁻⁴) grid consumption is ~1 % (survives). So R11's headline "silicate is not a nominal grid-life-limiter" is itself **conditional on the assumed 10⁻⁴**, i.e. on the unmeasured sub-100-nm PSD + cold operation. **Falsified** if grid consumption stays < 1 even at the Hsu/warm f_pass (silicate never limits regardless of PSD — R11's verdict unconditional).

**Honest expected reading (pre-registered):** K1, K2, K3 hold — the rejection factor is derived, spanning ~10⁻⁵ (cold bag + power-law-to-100 µm) to ~3×10⁻¹ (warm bag + Hsu nano-mode); R11's 10⁻⁴ is a favorable-corner number, not intrinsic. **The design rule that falls out:** the bag MUST run cold (sublimation-distillation at ≤ ~220 K, feasible per K2) to suppress gas entrainment; even then the electrostatic nano-tail leak (K4) is the residual bet. K5 holds — R11's benign grid-life verdict is conditional on the assumed rejection, so the whole bet-#2 grid-erosion "not a life-limiter" conclusion rests on one unmeasured input: **the sub-100-nm silicate inclusion PSD of ring ice + its grain charging.** That is this round's sharply-named pre-Gate-B measurement, and it is the physics *behind* R_silicate_contamination's "cannot resolve PSD from desk data" flag. ## Method

Deterministic, pure-Python. `run.py`:
1. **Vapor:** P_sat(T) = 10^(−2663.5/T + 12.537) Pa (Marti-Mauersberger 1993); J = P_sat/√(2π R_specific T); ρ_gas = P_sat·M/(R T); c̄ = √(8 R T/π M); A_ice = ṁ/J.
2. **Cutoff:** d_max(T) = ρ_gas(T)·L/ρ_grain (St = 1, free-molecular relaxation).
3. **Carryover:** f_pass_gas = massfrac(PSD below radius d_max/2). Power-law massfrac(<r) = (r^(4−q) − r_min^(4−q))/(r_max^(4−q) − r_min^(4−q)); bimodal adds a nano-mode of mass fraction φ_nano below 100 nm. f_pass = max(gas, electrostatic residual).
4. **Grid loop:** silicate_to_grid = f_pass·0.003·M_prop; grid_consumed = silicate_to_grid / 16.7 kg (R11 mapping: 400 g → 2.4 % thickness); life-limited if > 1 over the burn.
5. Grade K1–K5.

**Anchors & sources:** ice sublimation vapor pressure — Marti & Mauersberger 1993 (GRL 20, 363), validated at 200/250/273 K. Silicate density 3000 kg/m³; work of adhesion ~0.06 J/m². Ring silicate content 0.3 % — Cassini (>99 % water, B-ring). **Sub-100-nm silica grains detected** in Saturn stream/ring-fall particles — Hsu et al. 2011 (Nature 471, 393) nanometre SiO₂; Hsu et al. 2018 (Science 362) ring-inflow dust — but **the inclusion size distribution *within* main-ring ice particles is not directly measured** (stated explicitly, no source, as the load-bearing gap). Grid-wear mapping — R11 (NSTAR 30,352 h / 235 kg Xe; silicate:Xe sputter 3.5×; 400 g → 2.4 % grid thickness). ṁ and M_prop — matrix RF-ion inbound / Round F H₂/O₂ (690 t). All constants echoed into the result JSON.
