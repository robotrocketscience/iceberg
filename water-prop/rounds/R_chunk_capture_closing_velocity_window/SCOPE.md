## The question

Bet #1's containment sub-step degrades with closing velocity (∝ v²). Two physics quantities bound the operating point and neither has been derived:

1. **A FLOOR on achievable closing velocity** — the lowest relative velocity at the contact point set by ring dynamics you cannot navigate away: the chunk's own spin surface velocity ω×r (you contact a tumbling surface), Keplerian/Hill shear across the capture envelope, local velocity dispersion, and GNC control deadband. This is the *best case* — you cannot close slower than this without despinning.
2. **A CEILING on survivable closing velocity** — the highest relative velocity the containment structure (bag fabric + cinch) can absorb without tearing, ½·m·v² through the fabric's strain-energy capacity (energy limit) and the peak-deceleration membrane tension (tear/force limit), mass-scaled from fabric mechanics.

**If floor > ceiling for a given chunk mass, there is no feasible operating point — bet #1 is falsified for that mass independent of any reliability guess.** If floor ≪ ceiling, a window exists and containment failure (R-A14's 0.55 at low-m/s) is an *operational/GNC* effect, not a *structural* wall — a very different (and demonstrator-retirable) risk.

**H-J2 [S] — the containment bag-tear ceiling is m/s-to-tens-of-m/s and scales as ~m^(−1/6).** The mass-scaled ceiling = min(energy, force) is 5–30 m/s for 1–200 t under strong-fabric whole-bag anchors, decreasing weakly with mass (bag area ∝ r² ∝ m^(2/3), so v ∝ √(m^(2/3)/m) = m^(−1/6)). Under the pessimistic contact-patch + lightweight-fabric anchor it is ≥ 1.5 m/s for all masses. **Falsified** if the pessimistic ceiling < 1 m/s for any mass (structural wall reaches the achievable floor) or the strong-fabric ceiling > 100 m/s for 200 t (contradicts R_HE_graze order-of-magnitude).

**H-J3 [S, LOAD-BEARING] — a feasible window exists at every chunk mass; the floor sits ≥ 100× below the bag-tear ceiling.** ceiling/floor ≥ 100 at p50 spin for all four masses, and ≥ 10 even at the p95 spin tail under the pessimistic ceiling. **Therefore containment is not structurally/energy-limited at achievable closing velocities**, and R-A14's low-m/s containment degradation (0.55) is an operational/GNC reliability effect (bounce-out, alignment, partial mis-catch), not a materials wall. **Falsified** if ceiling/floor < 10 for any mass at p50 (window marginal or absent — bet #1 would face a hard structural wall).

**H-J4 [W] — chunk fragmentation, not bag tear, is the lower sub-ceiling for weak ice, but yields contained rubble.** The chunk-coherence threshold at weak ring ice (1 kPa tensile) is ~1–5 m/s (below the bag-tear ceiling), so a low-m/s catch may fragment the chunk — but the bag contains the rubble and water is still captured; coherent-chunk delivery, not water capture, is what degrades. **Falsified** if the 1 kPa fragmentation threshold > the bag-tear ceiling for all masses (fragmentation never binds first), or < the p50 floor for any mass (the chunk would shatter even at the gentlest achievable closing velocity — a real capture problem, not a cosmetic one).

**H-J5 [W] — despin is not a structural requirement.** Because even the un-despun p95 spin floor stays ≥ 10× below the pessimistic bag-tear ceiling for all masses, despinning the chunk is not required to survive containment structurally (it may still improve catch reliability/alignment). **Falsified** if the p95 (or fast-rotator) floor reaches within 10× of the pessimistic ceiling for any mass — which would make despin a hard structural prerequisite, not just a reliability aid.

## Method

Deterministic, pure-Python/numpy. `run.py`:
1. **mass→radius:** r = (3m/4πρ)^(1/3), ρ = 500 kg/m³ ring-aggregate (porous; larger r → larger spin AND larger bag; solid-ice 917 as a sensitivity).
2. **Floor:** v_spin = ω·r at ω ∈ {p50, p95, fast} from tier_0_5 (1–10 m bin: 0.01 / 0.05 / 0.28 rpm); v_shear = (3/2)·Ω·Δr, Ω = 2π/11.0 h, Δr = capture envelope (2·r); v_disp = 1 mm/s (up to 5); v_gnc = 1 mm/s (conops Hill-drift anchor). Floor = RSS; despun sets v_spin = 0.
3. **Ceiling:** energy v=√(2·U·σ_areal·A_bag·f_part/m); U ∈ {Zylon 6.6e4, Kevlar 3.0e4, lightweight 1.5e4} J/kg, σ_areal ∈ {0.3, 0.1} kg/m², A_bag = k·4πr² (k=2), f_part ∈ {1.0 whole-bag, π r²/A_bag contact-patch}. Force v=√(2·s·N_cap·P/m), N_cap = σ_areal·(σ_ult/ρ_fabric), P = 2π·r_bag, stroke s = r. Ceiling = min(energy, force).
4. **Fragmentation:** v_frag = √(2·s·σ_ice·π r²/m), σ_ice ∈ {1, 10, 100} kPa.
5. **Window:** ratio = ceiling/floor per (mass, spin-pct, despin, ceiling-pessimism); grade H-J1..5.

**Anchors & sources:** Cassini spin prior — tier_0_5_spin_prior.md (Spilker 2006; Morishima 2010/2012; Ohtsuki 2005). Ω, ring geometry — R_HE_graze / R_bring_fine_structure. Fabric mechanics — Zylon PBO (Toyobo datasheet: 5.8 GPa UTS, 3.5% strain, 1.54 g/cc), Kevlar-49 (DuPont: 3.6 GPa, 2.4%, 1.44 g/cc). Ring-ice tensile strength — cometary/rubble-pile analog 1–100 kPa (weak aggregate). All constants echoed into the result JSON.
