# R-eccentric-harvest-departure — does an eccentric harvest orbit rescue the megawatt departure floor?

**Status:** SCOPE pre-registered 2026-07-22, before `run.py`. `scope_bounds.py` computes the ring relative-velocity vs eccentricity and the reactor-floor brackets.
**Worker:** worktree-115637 session. Project-owner-directed: run the option-4 escape that `R-lowthrust-spiral-departure` (`2690b6b`) named but did not quantify.
**Predecessors under test:** `R-lowthrust-spiral-departure` (`2690b6b`, this session — electric continuous-spiral departure is 21.9 km/s → ~1248 kWe) and `R179 R_departure_anchor_reconciliation` (impulsive 8.5 km/s → 175 kWe). This round tests whether departing from an eccentric (periapsis-at-ring) harvest orbit — or via periapsis-concentrated burns — buys the departure Δv/power back down, and at what cost.

## The two assumptions under test

1. **Harvest-from-eccentric.** "Depart from near-escape energy by harvesting on an eccentric orbit (periapsis at the ring, apoapsis high)." This round argues it is **physically impossible**: gentle bag-harvest requires velocity-matching the ring particles (which orbit at v_circ ≈ 18.8 km/s), and capture works only at mm/s–low-m/s closing velocity (locked finding 1). An eccentric orbit's periapsis velocity exceeds v_circ by v_p − v_circ, which reaches **km/s** for any meaningful eccentricity — a hypervelocity ring encounter, not harvesting. The same variable (periapsis velocity) that cheapens departure destroys harvesting.
2. **Depart-circular-via-periapsis-burns.** Harvest on the (required) circular orbit, then depart via **periapsis-concentrated** low-thrust burns rather than a continuous spiral. Every burn is at r_p where velocity is high, so this **recovers most of the Oberth benefit** (Δv → ~8.5 km/s, not the 21.9 km/s continuous spiral) — meaning Round C's 1248 kWe assumed a needlessly pessimistic (continuous-thrust) profile. The catch this round quantifies: periapsis burning fires only near periapsis (low duty cycle, worsening as apoapsis grows toward escape), so the **peak** reactor power = average / duty stays elevated.

## Boundary

- **IN:** ring relative velocity v_p(r_a) − v_circ vs the m/s capture threshold (H-D1); a discrete periapsis-burn departure simulation from the circular ring orbit (constant thrust fired only within r < 2·r_p, raising apoapsis pass-by-pass to escape + v∞), reporting total Δv, elapsed time, firing time, revs, and **peak reactor power** (0.5·F·v_e/η) as a function of thrust; the minimum peak power that completes the departure within a 2-yr window; comparison to R179 (175 kWe impulsive) and Round C (1248 kWe continuous spiral).
- **OUT:** the harvest mechanism throughput model (locked finding 1 owns the capture-velocity threshold; this round only uses it as the harvest-orbit constraint); propellant-mass depletion during the burn (chunk 100 t dominates; m held ≈ const, flagged); a full continuous-thrust optimal-control trajectory (the periapsis-impulse approximation brackets it); the inbound leg.

## Pre-registered hypotheses (numeric, falsifiable, before run.py)

**H-D1 [S] — harvest-from-eccentric is killed by ring relative velocity.** The periapsis velocity excess v_p(r_a) − v_circ reaches ≥ 1 km/s (≥ 1000× the ~m/s capture threshold, locked finding 1) for apoapsis ≥ ~1.3× r_p, and ≥ 6 km/s near-parabolic. So the harvest orbit must be near-circular; harvesting from an eccentric orbit is a hypervelocity ring impact. **Falsified** if v_p − v_circ stays < 0.1 km/s out to apoapsis ≥ 2× r_p (would make eccentric harvest gentle).

**H-D2 [S] — periapsis-burn departure from the circular orbit recovers Oberth.** The simulated total departure Δv via periapsis-concentrated burns is **8.5–12 km/s** (vs the 21.9 km/s continuous spiral), because every burn is at r_p. **Falsified** if the simulated Δv > 15 km/s (no Oberth recovery) or < 8 km/s (below the impulsive floor, unphysical).

**H-D3 [S] — but the peak-power / duty-cycle penalty keeps the floor ≥ 300 kWe.** The minimum peak reactor power that completes the periapsis-burn departure within a 2-yr window is **≥ 300 kWe** (predicted 300–900 kWe) — below Round C's 1248 (continuous spiral is pessimistic) but well above R179's 175 (impulsive/chemical) — because the duty cycle collapses as apoapsis approaches escape (the classic low-thrust-escape many-revs tail). **Falsified** if the min peak power for a 2-yr departure is < 250 kWe (option 4 rescues to near R179) or > 1248 (worse than continuous, unphysical).

**H-D4 [W] — option 4 does not relieve bet #3 to Kilopower class.** No harvest-compatible orbit + departure strategy gets the departure reactor floor below ~300 kWe: eccentric harvest is impossible (H-D1), and the best electric departure (periapsis-burn from circular) is high-hundreds-kWe (H-D3). **Falsified** if some strategy yields a harvest-compatible departure floor < 250 kWe without a chemical stage.

**Honest expected reading (pre-registered):** H-D1/D2/D3 hold. Option 4 splits: the *harvest-on-eccentric* half is killed by ring relative velocity (clean); the *periapsis-burn departure* half is a **real partial win that corrects Round C** — the honest electric departure floor is ~300–900 kWe (periapsis-burn), not 1248 (continuous spiral) and not 175 (impulsive/chemical). This is the intellectually honest correction: Round C assumed the worst thrust profile; the best electric profile is periapsis-burn, and its floor is still fantasy-adjacent but not megawatt.

## Method

Deterministic. `run.py`: (1) v_p(r_a) − v_circ over apoapsis r_a ∈ [r_p, R_TITAN] vs the capture threshold. (2) Periapsis-burn departure sim: from the circular ring orbit, constant thrust F fired within r < 2·r_p each pass; update periapsis velocity (Oberth burn at r_p), apoapsis, elapsed (Σ period), firing (Σ arc time), until escape then add the v∞ completion; report Δv, elapsed, revs, peak power 0.5·F·v_e/η.
