## The assumption under test

R179 computes the departure reactor power from a **continuous-thrust electric** powerplant (thrust = 2P/v_e, burn spread over ~2 years) but uses a departure **Δv of 9.0 km/s** that is the **impulsive single-burn** value (`seqs["single"] = hyp_v(R_ring, v∞) − v_circ ≈ 8.5 km/s`, R179 `run.py:56`; the 9.0 in `run.py:117` is that value rounded up). An impulsive burn from the circular B-ring is Oberth-efficient — it fires at the full circular speed (18.8 km/s) deep in the well, so escaping to v∞ = 6.21 km/s costs only ~8.5 km/s. **A low-thrust electric thruster cannot do that.** It must spiral out of the well over many revolutions, and a tangential quasi-circular spiral from a circular orbit to escape costs Δv ≈ **v_circ ≈ 18.8 km/s** (the standard low-thrust-escape result — you shed the entire orbital velocity as the orbit expands to infinity; there is no Oberth concentration). So R179's floor pairs an Oberth Δv with a non-Oberth propulsion system — a physical inconsistency this round argues roughly **doubles the Δv and, through the rocket equation, multiplies the reactor floor several-fold.** This is the same impulsive-vs-continuous error axis 04 already caught on the *inbound* leg ("2.1-4.3× wrong"), not yet applied to the *departure* leg.
